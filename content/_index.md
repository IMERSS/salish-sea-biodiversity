### Tracking biodiversity change in the Salish Sea

The Salish Sea Biodiversity Atlas brings together datasets, analyses and tools for understanding how biodiversity is
changing across the Salish Sea. Building on more than a decade of research, the projects and datasets mapped here
draw on historical collections, published records, community science observations and targeted fieldwork to
establish regional baselines and to ask where, and how quickly, species are being gained or lost.

### Rooted in community

Detecting biodiversity change depends on knowledge held in local communities. Our work brings community-based
projects, regional non-profits and academic institutions together to develop practical, transparent methods that
communities can adopt to monitor biodiversity where they live, beginning with a
[protocol for detecting local extinction](https://imerss.github.io/detecting-local-extinction/docs/). Through this
work, we aim to strengthen the community foundations of biodiversity monitoring, linking local observation with
regional and national efforts to detect, understand and respond to biodiversity change.

### An open and evolving atlas

This atlas is a work in progress rather than a finished account. A synthesis of results across these projects and
datasets is in preparation, and new work will be added as it develops. We welcome contributions from researchers,
naturalists, First Nations, community organizations and stewardship groups across the Salish Sea, whether
observations, datasets, expertise or questions worth pursuing together.

Explore the map, or browse the [projects](projects/) and [data](data/) behind it. To contribute or propose a
collaboration, contact [IMERSS](https://www.imerss.org/) or
[open an issue](https://github.com/IMERSS/salish-sea-biodiversity/issues).
